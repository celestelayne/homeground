# The Commune Brief

**Status: Specification. No milestone owns it yet.**

## Purpose

The brief is what HomeGround sells. It is a document about one commune, bought
before a visit, that assembles what the French state and its statistical
agencies publish about that place — translated, dated, sourced, and honest
about what is missing.

It costs €9 because it should be bought at midnight while reading listings,
not budgeted for.

---

## The job it does

**"Is this place worth a plane ticket and three days?"**

That question is asked before anyone has seen a house, from a listing that
gives a commune and withholds an address. It is asked by someone who cannot
read French well enough to gather this themselves, does not know that a
*notaire* would surface half of it, and does not know what they do not know.

The brief answers by putting the published record in one place. It does not
answer whether they should go. That remains theirs.

---

## What it is, and what it is not

The brief **is** an assembly of the public record: what a commune is
designated as exposed to, what has been declared there, what its weather has
been, what the census counts, what the health directory places there, and where
every one of those came from.

The brief **is not**:

- a score, a rating, a grade, or a ranking of communes
- a recommendation to buy, visit, or avoid
- a statement that anywhere is safe or unsafe
- a survey, a valuation, or a substitute for a *notaire*
- a prediction about anything

`docs/methodology.md` governs this and does not bend for a paid product. A
document somebody paid for is *more* dangerous when it overstates, not less:
it is printed, forwarded, and believed.

---

## Subject and moment

**One commune**, identified by its INSEE code.

Not a property. HomeGround holds nothing at property level until listing
partnerships arrive, and a brief that implied otherwise would be selling a
precision it does not have.

**Bought before a visit.** That fixes the register: orientation, not diligence.
Enough to decide whether to go, not enough to decide whether to buy.

---

## Fixity

A brief is **rendered once, at purchase, and stored as rendered.**

Every figure in it is dated and attributed, and those dates must stay true. A
document that quietly re-renders from live data is a document whose "as at"
line lies the first time a source updates. The buyer keeps what they paid for.

A brief therefore carries the date it was made, and says that the sources
behind it continue to change.

---

## Structure

In this order, because it is the order the questions arrive in.

**1. What kind of place this is.** The photograph, the commune's name and
administrative context, its population, area and density, the class INSEE puts
it in, and its share of second homes. One page that answers "what am I looking
at".

**2. What the weather has been.** The last five complete years, month by month
rather than the four figures the free panel shows, with each station named,
its distance and its altitude. Winter light, mild mornings, summer heat, rain.

**3. What the state records.** Every designation with its sub-kinds, in English
with the French the state wrote beside it, and how many communes carry the
same. Every declared disaster — the whole history, not the recent window — with
the date the event began, the date the order was signed, and the order's
reference. Radon on its published scale.

**4. What is here.** The pharmacies and hospitals the health directory places
in the commune, with their addresses and how precisely each is located.

**5. What HomeGround could not find out.** See below.

**6. Sources and methodology.** Every source used, its publisher, its update
cadence, its coverage, its licence, and its stated limitations. The same
registry the figures cite, so the two cannot describe different sets.

---

## The Unknowns page

Section 5 is the section no competitor writes, and it is the reason to trust
the other four.

It lists, for this commune, what HomeGround looked for and could not report:

- fire history, because the state's disaster archive excludes fire by
  construction and HomeGround does not yet hold the fire database
- a sunshine figure from nearby, where the nearest station measuring it is
  forty kilometres away and a thousand metres up
- anything the archive does not carry for this commune at all
- what no source publishes for anywhere: whether a tradesman answers the phone,
  whether a doctor takes new patients, how a village feels in February

Written per commune, from the evidence states already held. An Unknown is a
finding, and this page is where the contract's insistence on that becomes
something a buyer can see.

---

## Its relationship to the free panel

The panel earns the click; the brief earns the €9.

The panel answers *what* — five figures and a station name. The brief answers
*how you would check it*: the order reference, the station's altitude, the
month-by-month table, the source's own limitations. A reader who wants to
verify a claim can, without reading French or knowing which agency to ask.

Nothing in the brief is withheld from the panel to make the brief worth buying.
The panel shows what a panel can hold. The brief is longer, fixed, printable,
and takeable to somebody else.

---

## Invariants

- a brief is about exactly one commune, identified by INSEE code
- every figure carries its source, the date that source observed it, and where
  it was borrowed from if it was borrowed
- every brief carries the date it was rendered, and says its sources continue
  to change
- a rendered brief is never silently regenerated
- an Unknown appears as an Unknown, and the Unknowns are collected in one place
- no score, rating, ranking, recommendation, or statement of safety appears
- nothing appears in a brief that could not be traced back to a registered
  source

---

## Out of scope for the first version

- property-level anything
- comparison between communes
- travel times, which need routing
- prices, which need the comparability decision in `docs/methodology.md`
- translation into any language but English
- delivery, payment, accounts and licensing, which are commerce rather than
  content

---

## Open questions

- **Whether the brief prompts questions.** "A notaire would ask about the
  differential settlement designation" is useful and is advice. Leaving it out
  costs the buyer something real; putting it in starts HomeGround down a road
  `docs/methodology.md` closes. Unresolved.
- **How long it should be.** Six sections could be four pages or twelve. A
  document nobody finishes is worse than a shorter one.
- **Whether an old brief can be refreshed**, and whether that is a new purchase.
- **What happens when a source contradicts a brief already sold.** The dates
  make it defensible; it does not make it comfortable.
